Safety guide

Small habits.
Stronger protection.

You don’t need to be a tech expert. Start with a pause, a few checks, and the confidence to say no.

01 / PAUSE & VERIFY

Take the conversation somewhere you trust.

  1. Stop before clicking, paying or replying. A deadline in a message does not make it trustworthy.
  2. Open your bank or service’s official app yourself. Don’t use the link or number in the suspicious message.
  3. If someone you know asks for money, call them on a number you already have.
02 / KEEP CONTROL

Your codes are yours alone.

  1. Never share an OTP, password, card PIN, CVV or UPI PIN with someone who calls or messages you.
  2. Don’t install an APK or a remote-access app because a stranger says it is required for a refund or support.
  3. Use different passwords for important accounts and turn on two-step verification where available.
03 / NOTICE THE TACTICS

Pressure is part of the trick.

“Pay now.” “Keep this secret.” “Your account will close.” “You won a prize, but first pay a fee.” These are reasons to stop and verify independently.

A familiar logo, official-sounding name or polished message is not proof. Scam Shield can help you examine the wording; it cannot authenticate the sender.

04 / ACT QUICKLY

Sent money? Contact your bank immediately.

Use its official app, website or the number on your card. Ask about blocking affected payment access and reporting the transaction.

For financial cyber fraud in India, call 1930 or report at cybercrime.gov.in ↗.

  1. Save screenshots, transaction IDs, phone numbers and the timeline. Don’t delete evidence.
  2. If you shared a password, change it from a trusted device and review active sessions.
  3. Don’t pay anyone promising a guaranteed recovery. Reporting promptly may help, but recovery is not guaranteed.
